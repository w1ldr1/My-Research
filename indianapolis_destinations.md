# Popular Destinations Within 50 Miles of Indianapolis, Indiana

---

## Parks & Nature Areas

1. **Eagle Creek Park** (~10 miles NW)
   - One of the largest municipal parks in the U.S. at 3,900 acres, surrounding a 1,400-acre reservoir.
   - Sailing, kayaking, birdwatching, swimming beach, Go Ape Zipline course.

2. **White River State Park** (Downtown Indianapolis)
   - America's only urban cultural state park, spanning 250 acres in the city's heart.
   - Houses the Indianapolis Zoo, Eiteljorg Museum, scenic Canal Walk, and formal gardens.

3. **Brown County State Park** (~45 miles S)
   - Indiana's largest state park, nicknamed the "Little Smokies" for dramatic ridges and deep ravines.
   - Famous for stunning fall foliage, rugged hiking and mountain biking trails, and a historic lodge.

4. **Mounds State Park** (~40 miles NE)
   - Features 10 earthworks built by prehistoric Adena and Hopewell peoples for religious/astronomical ceremonies.
   - Hiking trails wind along bluffs overlooking the White River.

5. **Fort Harrison State Park** (~12 miles NE)
   - Former U.S. Army post converted into a lush park with scenic walking/biking trails, golf course.
   - Museum of 20th Century Warfare; beloved for rolling hills and "Warbler Woods" nature preserve.

6. **Morgan-Monroe State Forest** (~35 miles S)
   - Over 24,000 acres of rugged forested terrain.
   - Primitive camping, backcountry hiking, and the scenic three-lakes hiking loop.

7. **Holiday Park** (~7 miles N)
   - Famous for "The Ruins" — repurposed architectural facades from a demolished New York skyscraper.
   - Wooded trails, modern nature center, and top-rated playgrounds along the White River.

8. **Cataract Falls State Recreation Area** (~45 miles SW)
   - Home to Indiana's largest waterfall by volume, with upper and lower falls on Mill Creek.
   - Historically preserved covered bridge and easy hiking paths.

9. **Meltzer Woods Nature Preserve** (~30 miles SE)
   - One of Indiana's last fragments of original old-growth forest; trees dating back to the 1600s.
   - Quiet 1.2-mile trail offering a glimpse of pre-settlement Indiana landscape.

## Historical Sites

10. **Conner Prairie** (~20 miles NE, Fishers)
    - Nationally recognized living history museum recreating life in 1836 Indiana and the Civil War era.
    - Tethered helium balloon rides, farm animals, 19th-century village.

11. **Soldiers' and Sailors' Monument** (Downtown Indianapolis)
    - Iconic 284-foot centerpiece of Monument Circle, honoring Hoosiers who served in the Civil and Spanish-American Wars.
    - Elevator/stairway to observation deck with 360-degree city views.

12. **Benjamin Harrison Presidential Site** (~2 miles N)
    - Preserved 1875 Victorian home of the 23rd U.S. President.
    - Original furnishings, political artifacts, and exhibits on Harrison's presidency.

13. **Lockerbie Square Historic District** (Just E of Downtown)
    - Indianapolis's oldest intact residential neighborhood with cobblestone streets and restored 19th-century cottages.
    - Also home to the James Whitcomb Riley Museum House.

14. **The Hoosier Gym** (~35 miles E, Knightstown)
    - Iconic gymnasium used as the filming location for the movie *Hoosiers*.
    - Preserved in its 1950s state; open for tours and community basketball games.

15. **Indiana War Memorial Plaza** (Downtown Indianapolis)
    - Massive park and building complex honoring veterans; headquarters for the American Legion.
    - Main building modeled after the Mausoleum of Halicarnassus; features stunning gold-leafed Shrine Room.

## Museums & Arts

16. **The Children's Museum of Indianapolis** (~3 miles N)
    - The largest children's museum in the world.
    - Dinosaur skeletons, planetarium, Sports Legends Experience; top family destination in the Midwest.

17. **Newfields (Indianapolis Museum of Art)** (~5 miles NW)
    - 152-acre campus with major art museum, historic Lilly House mansion, and "100 Acres" outdoor sculpture park.
    - One of the most complete art-and-nature experiences in the region.

18. **Eiteljorg Museum of American Indians and Western Art** (Downtown Indianapolis)
    - One of only two museums east of the Mississippi devoted to both Native American culture and Western American art.
    - World-class collections of beadwork, pottery, and paintings by artists such as Georgia O'Keeffe.

19. **Indianapolis Motor Speedway Museum** (~6 miles W, Speedway)
    - World's most famous 2.5-mile oval track with on-site museum housing dozens of Indy 500-winning cars.
    - Track tours typically available (verify current access, as building was under renovation in 2025).

20. **Indiana Medical History Museum** (~3 miles W)
    - Housed in the 1890s Old Pathology Building of the former Central State Hospital.
    - Oldest surviving pathology facility in the U.S.; original labs, autopsy amphitheater, historic medical library.

21. **Indiana State Museum** (Downtown Indianapolis)
    - Tells the story of Indiana from prehistoric times to the present.
    - Building incorporates materials from all 92 Indiana counties; features the state's largest IMAX theater.

## Cities & Towns

22. **Zionsville** (~15 miles NW)
    - Charming town with brick-paved Main Street lined with boutiques, art galleries, and fine dining.
    - Known for well-preserved architecture and upscale small-town atmosphere.

23. **Nashville, Indiana** (~44 miles S)
    - Quaint village in the hills of Brown County, filled with craft galleries, unique shops, and candy stores.
    - An artist colony since the early 1900s; gateway to Brown County State Park.

24. **Columbus, Indiana** (~46 miles S)
    - Internationally recognized for its collection of modernist architecture by I.M. Pei, Eero Saarinen, and others.
    - Guided architectural tours available; scenic Mill Race Park.

25. **Franklin** (~25 miles S)
    - Lively historic downtown anchored by the 1920s Artcraft Theatre and classic courthouse square.
    - Home to Franklin College; growing scene of local breweries and coffee shops.

26. **Noblesville** (~25 miles NE)
    - Historic square with the 1870s Hamilton County Courthouse.
    - Nearby Ruoff Music Center (one of the country's busiest outdoor amphitheaters) and Strawtown Koteewi Park.

27. **Carmel Arts & Design District** (~15 miles N)
    - Home to the nationally acclaimed Palladium concert hall and Center for the Performing Arts.
    - Walkable downtown with galleries and restaurants; connected by the Monon Trail.

## Other Notable Attractions

28. **Indianapolis Zoo** (Downtown Indianapolis)
    - World-class institution accredited as a zoo, aquarium, and botanical garden on a 64-acre campus.
    - Known for the Simon Skjodt International Orangutan Center and interactive dolphin pavilion.

29. **The Monon Trail** (N from Downtown through suburbs)
    - 27-mile paved rail-to-trail greenway connecting downtown Indianapolis through Broad Ripple and Carmel up to Sheridan.
    - Major recreational corridor for cyclists, runners, and walkers.

30. **World's Largest Ball of Paint** (~48 miles NE, Alexandria)
    - Quirky roadside attraction: a single baseball covered in over 25,000 layers of paint since 1977.
    - Visitors can schedule an appointment to add their own layer.
