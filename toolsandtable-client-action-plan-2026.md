# Tools & Table Client Action Plan

Date: 2026-04-20
Purpose:
- client-facing summary of recommended work
- prioritized for launch readiness, credibility, and growth
- includes rough effort bands, not fixed estimates

## Executive Summary

Tools & Table has a strong brand foundation. The visual identity is distinctive, the tone is consistent, and the concept is clear. The current issues are mostly execution and launch-readiness issues rather than positioning problems.

The immediate goal should be to move the site from “promising early build” to “credible live publication.” That requires fixing indexing, removing unfinished navigation, improving metadata, replacing placeholder imagery, and strengthening trust signals.

## Recommended Priorities

### Priority 1: Launch Readiness

Goal:
- make the site indexable, navigable, and publicly credible

Includes:
- remove `noindex, nofollow`
- fix duplicate homepage title tag
- replace or remove dead `#` links
- add page-level metadata and social previews

Estimated effort:
- `4 to 8 hours`

Expected outcome:
- search engines can index the site
- users stop encountering obviously unfinished elements
- search and social previews look professional

### Priority 2: Credibility and Conversion

Goal:
- make the homepage and key pages feel trustworthy and complete

Includes:
- replace placeholder recipe images with real photography
- expand About page with story, mission, and methodology
- improve newsletter messaging and trust cues

Estimated effort:
- `6 to 12 hours`, depending on content and image readiness

Expected outcome:
- stronger first impression
- better newsletter conversion
- clearer proof of expertise

### Priority 3: Information Architecture

Goal:
- give the brand’s language real destinations and browsing paths

Includes:
- create `Start Here`
- create `Old World`, `New World`, and `Techniques` landing pages
- improve internal linking between recipes, tools, and educational content

Estimated effort:
- `8 to 16 hours`

Expected outcome:
- visitors can browse intentionally instead of hitting dead ends
- the brand structure becomes clearer
- long-term SEO performance improves

### Priority 4: Technical and Quality Cleanup

Goal:
- reduce maintenance issues and improve quality baseline

Includes:
- consolidate duplicate font loading
- clean head output
- add schema
- run accessibility pass

Estimated effort:
- `6 to 10 hours`

Expected outcome:
- cleaner theme output
- stronger search and accessibility foundation
- fewer preventable quality issues post-launch

## Suggested Project Phasing

### Phase 1: Immediate Launch Fixes

Timeline:
- `1 to 3 business days`

Work:
- remove `noindex`
- fix metadata
- resolve dead links
- correct duplicate title output

This phase should happen first. There is limited value in adding more content while the site is still blocking indexing and exposing unfinished links.

### Phase 2: Trust and Conversion

Timeline:
- `2 to 5 business days`

Work:
- add real recipe imagery
- rewrite About page
- improve newsletter section

This phase improves how the site feels to first-time visitors and increases the chance that traffic converts into subscribers.

### Phase 3: Structure for Growth

Timeline:
- `3 to 7 business days`

Work:
- build hub pages
- improve internal linking
- align homepage pathways with actual content destinations

This phase makes the brand easier to understand and supports future growth in both traffic and engagement.

## Recommended Team Split

### Developer

Responsible for:
- indexing and metadata fixes
- template updates
- dead link cleanup
- featured image logic
- technical cleanup

### Content Lead or Founder

Responsible for:
- approving homepage copy
- approving About page content
- defining editorial standards
- shaping newsletter promise

### Designer or Creative Lead

Responsible for:
- recipe image selection or treatment
- CTA hierarchy
- social preview image
- mobile polish review

## Risks If Work Is Delayed

- search engines may continue to ignore the site
- visitors may read the site as unfinished
- dead-end navigation may weaken trust quickly
- the newsletter may underperform even if traffic improves
- content growth may outpace site structure, making later cleanup slower

## Practical Recommendation

If budget or time is limited, do this first:

1. Remove `noindex` and fix metadata.
2. Remove all dead links.
3. Replace placeholder imagery.
4. Improve the About page.
5. Improve the newsletter section.

That is the minimum set of changes most likely to improve both credibility and growth.

## Rough Budget Framing

If this is handled by a WordPress freelancer or small agency, a realistic scope could be framed as:

- launch-fix sprint: `half day to 1 day`
- credibility and conversion sprint: `1 to 2 days`
- content architecture sprint: `1 to 2 days`
- technical cleanup sprint: `1 day`

Actual cost will depend on whether imagery, copy approval, and page structure decisions are already settled.

## Bottom Line

Tools & Table does not need a brand reset. It needs finishing work in the places visitors notice first. Once the indexing, link, metadata, imagery, and trust issues are addressed, the site will be much closer to a strong live editorial brand rather than an attractive draft.
